# NZ Commercial Finance Adviser Interview Guide

## Purpose

Learn how commercial finance advisers currently prepare and manage private-credit applications. Understand their real workflow before deciding what to build.

Allow around 20–30 minutes. Ask about anonymised examples only; do not request confidential borrower information.

## Opening

> We’re researching how commercial finance applications work in New Zealand. We’re not selling anything, and we don’t need confidential client information. Could you walk us through your process using an anonymised example?

## Current process

1. What kinds of finance deals do you work on most often? For example, property finance, working capital or equipment finance.
2. Can you walk me through a recent deal, from the first borrower conversation to the lender’s decision? Please use an anonymised example.
3. What information and documents do you usually collect from a borrower?
4. How do you decide which lenders to approach?
5. Where do you find out what each lender is currently willing to consider?
6. How do you prepare and send an application to a lender?
7. What happens after you submit it? How do you handle lender questions, missing documents and status updates?

## Difficult or time-consuming work

8. Which part of that process takes the most time?
9. What do you find yourself copying, reformatting or entering more than once?
10. What commonly causes delays, rework or an application to be declined?
11. How do you keep track of active deals and follow-ups?
12. What software, spreadsheets or other tools do you use for this today? What works well, and what still requires manual work?

## Test the product idea

After hearing about their process, briefly explain:

> We’re exploring a subscription tool that helps advisers organise application documents, prepare a draft deal summary, compare a deal with lender criteria and track progress. The adviser reviews the work and chooses which lenders to approach.

Then ask:

13. Which part of that would be useful in your work, if any?
14. Which part would you not trust a tool to do?
15. What would the tool need to show so you could check whether a possible lender match is relevant?
16. What concerns would you have about putting borrower or guarantor information into it?
17. What tools or processes would it need to work alongside for you to consider using it?

## Willingness to pay and next steps

18. How many deals do you typically handle in a month?
19. If a tool saved time on this process, who in your business would decide whether to pay for it?
20. Have you paid for similar software before? What made it worth paying for, or not worth paying for?
21. What would you need to see in a demo or trial before considering a monthly subscription?
22. Would you be willing to try a prototype using fictional data and give us feedback?
23. Is there another adviser or lender you think we should speak to?

## Interview tip

Don’t lead with “Would you use this?” People may say yes to be polite. Their recent examples, existing workarounds and willingness to try a prototype are more useful evidence.
