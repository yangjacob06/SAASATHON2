# NZ Commercial Finance Adviser Interview Guide

## Purpose

Learn how commercial finance advisers currently prepare and manage private-credit applications. Understand their real workflow before deciding what to build.

Allow around 20–30 minutes. Ask about anonymised examples only; do not request confidential borrower information.

Some questions overlap with others. Use the follow-ups where they fit naturally; you do not need to ask every question if the adviser has already covered it.

## Opening

> We’re researching how commercial finance applications work in New Zealand. We’re not selling anything, and we don’t need confidential client information. Could you walk us through your process using an anonymised example?

## Current process

1. What kinds of finance deals do you work on most often? For example, property finance, working capital or equipment finance.
2. Can you walk me through a recent deal, from the first borrower conversation to the lender’s decision? Please use an anonymised example.
3. What information and documents do you usually collect from a borrower?
4. Thinking across a typical deal, what kinds of information and documents might you handle—for example, company and guarantor details, financial statements, forecasts, bank statements, existing debts, security valuations, contracts or invoices? Which file types do these usually arrive as, and which are essential for understanding and summarising a deal?
5. Which documents or information do you work with most often, and which are the hardest or most time-consuming to use?
6. How do you decide which lenders to approach?
7. Where do you find out what each lender is currently willing to consider?
8. Who keeps your information about lender criteria up to date, and how often does it change?
9. How do you prepare and send an application to a lender?
10. What happens after you submit it? How do you handle lender questions, missing documents and status updates?

## Difficult or time-consuming work

11. Which part of that process takes the most time?
12. What do you find yourself copying, reformatting or entering more than once?
13. What commonly causes delays, rework or an application to be declined?
14. How do you keep track of active deals and follow-ups?
15. What software, spreadsheets or other tools do you use for this today? What works well, and what still requires manual work?

## Test the product idea

After hearing about their process, briefly explain:

> We’re exploring a subscription tool that helps advisers organise application documents, prepare a draft deal summary, compare a deal with lender criteria and track progress. The adviser reviews the work and chooses which lenders to approach.

Then ask:

16. For a first version, would it be useful to enter or paste deal information, or would uploading documents be essential?
17. What exact sections or details would you expect a useful deal summary to include?
18. What should the tool do when information is missing or two documents show different figures?
19. Which part of the product idea would be useful in your work, if any?
20. Which parts would you not trust a tool to do?
21. What would the tool need to show so you could check whether a possible lender match is relevant?
22. What would you need to verify in a generated summary before sharing it with a lender?
23. What concerns would you have about putting borrower or guarantor information into it?
24. What tools or processes would it need to work alongside for you to consider using it?

## Willingness to pay and next steps

25. How many deals do you typically handle in a month?
26. If a tool saved time on this process, who in your business would decide whether to pay for it?
27. Have you paid for similar software before? What made it worth paying for, or not worth paying for?
28. What would you need to see in a demo or trial before considering a monthly subscription?
29. Would you be willing to try a prototype using fictional data and give us feedback?
30. Is there another adviser or lender you think we should speak to?

## Interview tip

Don’t lead with “Would you use this?” People may say yes to be polite. Their recent examples, existing workarounds and willingness to try a prototype are more useful evidence.
